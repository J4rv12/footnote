# Away Travel — 2026/27 edition

## 1.1 How we travel

The team manager chooses the mode of travel for each away match by distance from the training ground. Journeys under 200 miles are by coach. Journeys between 200 and 350 miles are by train, with the coach meeting the squad at the station. Journeys over 350 miles are by chartered flight.

The mode of travel is confirmed when the travelling squad is announced. Players do not make their own way to away matches, by any means, unless the head coach agrees in advance.

## 1.2 The team coach

Seats on the team coach are allocated by the team manager. The front rows are for coaching and medical staff. The back seat belongs to the longest-serving players, who know this and remind everyone.

Feet stay off the seats. Music is played through headphones only. The coach driver has absolute authority over the coach, including over the head coach, and has used it.

## 1.3 Trains and flights

On trains, the club reserves a carriage for the travelling party. Players stay in the reserved carriage and do not upgrade, swap seats with the public or wander to the buffet car in club kit.

On chartered flights, players carry hand luggage only. Kit, boots and equipment travel in the hold with the kit manager. Passports are carried for every flight, including domestic ones, because the charter company asks and arguing with airports is not a good use of anyone's afternoon.

## 2.1 Departure times

Where the journey takes more than two and a half hours, the squad travels the day before and stays overnight. Overnight trips leave the training ground at 14:00 on the day before the match, unless the team manager announces a different time.

Same-day trips leave the training ground at the time on the travel sheet, which is issued with the squad announcement. The travel sheet is the only source of departure times. A time heard in the canteen is not a departure time.

## 2.2 Meeting points

The standard meeting point is the training ground car park at Gate B, 20 minutes before departure. Bags go in the coach hold, and players board when the team manager calls their name.

A player who lives near the route may be collected at Little Dithering services, by arrangement with the team manager at least 24 hours before departure. The coach stops there for two minutes, not for coffee.

## 2.3 Missing the coach

The coach leaves at the stated time and does not wait. A player who misses it travels at their own expense, by a route the team manager approves, and is treated as late under the Code of Conduct.

The coach has left without the captain twice. It will do so again if necessary.

## 3.1 Hotel room allocation

On overnight trips, players share rooms in pairs. The team manager allocates rooms and rotates the pairs every three trips, so that nobody shares with the same person all season.

Room requests are made to the team manager by the Thursday before the trip. The captain and any player returning from a long injury have a single room. Players do not swap rooms after allocation, even by mutual agreement, because the medical team needs to know where everyone is.

## 3.2 Hotel conduct

Room numbers are not shared with anyone outside the travelling party. No visitors are allowed in players' rooms at any time. The curfew in the Code of Conduct applies on every overnight trip.

Minibars are locked at the club's request before the squad arrives. Anything charged to a room, other than the room itself, is paid by the player, and the team manager reads the bill.

## 3.3 Checking out

On the morning of the match, players pack their bags before breakfast and leave them in the hotel lobby by 10:00. Keys are handed to the team manager, not to reception.

Anything left in a hotel room is collected by the kit manager only if it is club property. Personal items are the player's problem.

## 3.4 The pre-match walk

On the morning of an overnight away match, the squad takes a 20-minute walk near the hotel, led by the fitness coach. Everyone in the travelling squad takes part, including players who expect to start on the bench.

Players wear the club training top for the walk, so that staff can count them at a distance and so that nobody is mistaken for a member of the public and left behind. The walk is not an opportunity to visit shops.

## 3.5 Rest before the match

After the pre-match walk, players rest in their rooms until the team manager calls them down for departure to the ground. Rest means lying down with the curtains drawn, not gaming with the door open.

Players who cannot sleep may read, listen to music or watch television quietly. The medical team is available in its own room for anyone who needs treatment before departure.

## 4.1 Arrival at the away ground

The team manager contacts the home club's stadium staff before departure to confirm parking for the coach, the location of the away dressing room and any local restrictions.

On arrival, players stay on the coach until the team manager has checked the dressing room. Players then go straight to the away dressing room, carrying their own wash bags, and do not stop in the tunnel to talk to the home side.

## 4.2 Away reporting time

For away matches, players report to the away dressing room 2 h 45 min before kick-off. Reporting means changed into the club warm-up top, phone in its pouch and seated for the head coach's arrival.

The reporting time applies to every away fixture, whatever the kick-off time, including midweek matches and cup ties. The extra half hour compared with home matches allows for unfamiliar dressing rooms, missing pegs and the occasional cold shower.

## 4.3 Away dressing rooms

The away dressing room is left cleaner than the squad found it. The kit manager inspects it before the coach leaves, and the coach does not leave until the kit manager is satisfied.

Nothing is pinned or taped to the walls of an away dressing room, including team sheets, motivational quotes or the home side's own notices about where not to pin things.

## 4.4 Leaving the away ground

The coach leaves the away ground 60 minutes after the final whistle. Players named for interviews complete them first, and the coach waits for them. It does not wait for anyone else.

Players are on the coach, with their bags, by the time the team manager has finished counting. A player who is still in the shower when the count finishes is late, however clean they are.

## 4.5 The away end

At the final whistle of every away match, players in the matchday squad walk to the away end and applaud the travelling crowd, whatever the result. The travelling crowd paid for tickets, travel and in some cases a hotel to be there, and is thanked every time.

Players do not throw shirts, boots or shin pads into the crowd without the kit manager's agreement. Shirts thrown without it are charged at the replica price.

## 5.1 Daily expenses

Each player may claim daily expenses for away fixtures, up to a limit of £299 per player per day. Daily expenses cover incidental costs: taxis, laundry, phone charges and anything else the team manager agrees is a reasonable cost of being away from home for the club.

Hotel rooms, club travel and anything booked by the club are paid directly by the club and do not count towards the limit.

## 5.2 Claiming expenses

Expenses are claimed through the club app within 14 days of the match, with a photograph of the receipt for every item. No receipt, no refund.

The club does not refund alcohol, minibar charges, gambling, fines of any kind, or parking tickets collected while a player was "only popping out". Approved claims are paid with the following month's wages.

## 5.3 Lost property

Club property lost on an away trip is reported to the kit manager before the squad leaves the town. Personal property lost on an away trip is reported to the player's own insurer.

The club will make one phone call to a hotel or stadium on a player's behalf. It will not make a second.

## 6.1 Travel documents

Every player keeps a passport that is valid for at least six months from the start of each season. Where a visa is needed for a pre-season tour or a friendly abroad, the club arranges it, and the player provides whatever the club asks for by the date the club asks for it.

The team manager holds a copy of every passport. On trips abroad, players carry their own passports at all times; the team manager does not carry them for anyone.

## 6.2 Pre-season tours

Pre-season tours have their own travel sheet, issued at least two weeks before departure, and their own rules on free time, which the team manager announces at the first team meeting on tour.

Daily expenses on pre-season tours are not claimed under section 5.1. A separate tour allowance is set before each tour and paid in advance.

## 6.3 Illness while travelling

The club doctor travels with the squad on every away trip and carries a medical bag on the coach. A player who feels unwell tells the club doctor at once, not the team-mate in the next seat.

A player who is too unwell to play is kept apart from the rest of the squad where possible, including on the journey home. The club doctor decides whether the player travels home with the squad or separately.
